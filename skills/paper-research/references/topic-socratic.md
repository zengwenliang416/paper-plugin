# Topic Focusing (Socratic)

Turn a vague interest into a focused, researchable question. Ask one question at a time and converge — do not accept the first broad topic.

## Socratic prompts

- **Phenomenon**: what exactly puzzles you? what triggered the interest?
- **Known vs unknown**: what is already established? what is genuinely open?
- **Scope**: which population / setting / data / method bounds the question?
- **Contribution**: if answered, what changes, and for whom?
- **Feasibility**: can it be answered with available data, time, and skills?

## FINER test

A good question is **F**easible, **I**nteresting, **N**ovel, **E**thical, **R**elevant. Reject or reshape a question that fails any of these.

## PICO-style framing (when applicable)

Population/Problem · Intervention/Method · Comparison · Outcome — useful for empirical and clinical questions.

## Output

1–2 focused research questions + a one-line scope (what is in, what is out), ready for the review step. Avoid topics too broad to review or too narrow to matter.
